# Bible character series guide

Use this guide for `Reflections > 성경 인물`. The series reconstructs a person
from the biblical text without turning a few scenes into a complete personality
diagnosis.

## Editorial promise

- Begin with the label readers already know, then test whether the canonical
  passages support, complicate, or correct it.
- Let repeated actions and words form the profile. Do not invent childhood,
  motives, appearance, temperament, or private thoughts.
- Give the reader enough passage context to verify the interpretation.
- Separate three evidence levels throughout the post:
  1. `본문이 직접 말함`
  2. `문맥상 가능한 해석`
  3. `후대 전승`
- Treat silence as silence. `성경에 기록이 없다` is a useful conclusion.

## Source hierarchy

1. Canonical biblical passage in its immediate context
2. Parallel canonical passages
3. Language or historical reference that explains a word or setting
4. Later church tradition, labeled explicitly and kept outside the biblical
   profile

Do not use an apocryphal work or later legend to fill a canonical gap. A later
tradition may appear in a short boundary section only when readers commonly
encounter it.

## Translation and quotation

- Record the Korean translation used for every direct quotation.
- Use short quotations around the decisive phrase and summarize the surrounding
  scene. Link the full passage so the reader can check context.
- Do not splice separate verses into a sentence that sounds like one continuous
  quotation.
- When translations differ materially, describe the difference rather than
  silently choosing the wording that best supports the thesis.
- Preserve verse numbers and distinguish quotation from paraphrase.

## Recommended structure

Adapt the headings to the person; do not copy them mechanically.

1. Familiar label and one-sentence correction
2. Passage map: every canonical appearance relevant to the profile
3. Name and identity: what is known and what remains unknown
4. Scenes in narrative order, with context before interpretation
5. A pattern table connecting action, spoken words, and evidence level
6. A fair reading of the person's failure or tension
7. The strongest confession, change, or unresolved ending
8. `성경 밖에서 시작되는 이야기`: later tradition and its limits, only when
   useful
9. One concrete reading or reflection practice

## Analysis guardrails

- Do not reduce a person to the scene for which they are famous.
- Do not excuse an explicit failure merely to create a surprising reversal.
- Do not diagnose modern personality types, mental illness, trauma, or attachment
  styles from sparse narrative evidence.
- Do not claim a causal motive when the text only records an action.
- Compare the person's evidentiary situation with other characters before
  singling them out. For example, ask what others had already seen or heard.
- If the text offers two plausible readings, name both and state why the text
  cannot settle the choice.
- Do not write the later outcome back into an earlier scene as though the person
  already understood it.
- Keep theological claims proportionate to the passage and identify when an
  interpretation depends on a particular tradition.

## Series value

Each post should add a reusable artifact:

- a passage matrix;
- a timeline;
- a direct-statement/inference/tradition map; or
- a comparison with how parallel accounts frame the same person.

Store the artifact in the post's `artifacts/` directory. This is the
first-party contribution that makes the series more than a character summary.

## Final checks

- Every quoted phrase matches the named translation and linked passage.
- The article covers all canonical scenes material to its main claim.
- Direct statement, inference, and tradition remain visibly distinct.
- No unknown relative, motive, personality trait, or missionary route is stated
  as fact.
- Famous failure and less-famous faithful action are both represented.
- The conclusion gives a reading practice rather than a heroic moral lesson.
